# Portfolio Landing Page Generator

Treat this as a one-page website whose job is to quickly establish credibility, communicate the person's value, and drive one clear action. The page should feel personal, confident, and specific rather than generic.

## Start with the person and the goal

Before writing or coding, identify:
- Who the portfolio belongs to and what field they work in.
- What the primary audience should understand immediately.
- What the page should encourage visitors to do next.
- What proof is available: projects, testimonials, metrics, certifications, or experience.

If the brief is vague, choose a clear, believable identity and make the copy feel grounded in a real professional context.

## Recommended page structure

A strong portfolio landing page usually includes:
1. Hero section with a clear headline, short supporting text, and a primary call to action.
2. Featured work or selected projects with concise descriptions and visuals.
3. About section that explains approach, values, or specialty.
4. Services or expertise section with short, concrete bullets.
5. Social proof such as testimonials, logos, or measurable outcomes.
6. Contact section and footer with an easy next step.

Keep the page focused. One page should not try to do everything; it should guide the visitor toward the most important action.

## Writing guidance

Use copy that is specific, warm, and easy to scan.
- Lead with the person's specialty, not a vague slogan.
- Use plain language and short sentences.
- Replace filler phrases with concrete details.
- Make calls to action direct: "View work", "Book a call", "Hire me", or "Get in touch".
- Keep tone consistent with the profession and audience.

## Visual and UX guidance

Create a layout that feels polished and intentional.
- Make the hero the emotional center of the page.
- Use generous spacing and a clear visual hierarchy.
- Favor one strong accent color and restrained decorative elements.
- Highlight featured work with strong imagery, clean cards, or editorial layouts.
- Keep interactions simple and useful, especially on mobile.
- Ensure the experience is readable, accessible, and responsive across screen sizes.

## Implementation expectations

When generating the page, prefer:
- Semantic HTML sections and meaningful headings.
- Clear reusable components or CSS classes.
- Smooth, responsive layout behavior.
- Accessible contrast, keyboard focus states, and legible text.
- Performance-conscious assets and lightweight interactions.

If the user wants a full implementation, produce a polished single-page experience with content tailored to the person, industry, and goal.

## Output style

Deliver the result as:
- A concise plan for the page structure.
- Strong copy for each section.
- A visual direction with palette, typography, and layout notes.
- Responsive and accessible implementation details.
- Optional code for a landing page in HTML, CSS, or a frontend framework.
